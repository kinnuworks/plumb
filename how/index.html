How Plumb works

The problem

MUSD trades about 1% under $1. On Mezo's main MUSD/mUSDC pool, roughly 5.3 million MUSD is offered for sale between $0.99 and $1.00, and the buy side holds under $40,000. Until mid October a 100,000 MUSD sale cleared at about $0.99. Since then the bid side has thinned, and the same sale runs out of buyers.

The vault

You deposit mUSDC. The vault places it as a bid that starts one tick below the market and runs 2% deeper, so a seller meets real buyers instead of an empty book. The position is staked in the pool's gauge and earns MEZO, which streams to depositors.

MUSD the bid buys goes into sMUSD, Mezo's savings vault, and earns there while MUSD is under $1. Once MUSD trades within 0.2% of $1, the vault takes it back out and offers it for sale at $1 or more. That round trip is where depositors earn the discount.

What keeps it safe

  • There is no owner. Every parameter is fixed at deployment.
  • The vault only moves liquidity when the live price agrees with the 30-minute average, so one block of manipulation can't steer it.
  • Withdrawals pay your share of each token directly. No price is involved, so nobody can game an exit.
  • Bids and deposits stop if MUSD falls below $0.96, and bidding stops once 60% of the vault is MUSD.
  • Nothing relies on signatures or meta-transactions.

The sMUSD strategy

sMUSD keeps 7.38 million MUSD in its default strategy as plain MUSD. PlumbAskStrategy plugs into the same hook. It offers a capped slice only at $1.001 and up and keeps the rest idle, so withdrawals stay instant. Savers never sell below par. When the order fills and MUSD later dips, the strategy buys back more MUSD than it sold, and the surplus becomes sMUSD yield. Switching to it takes one call from the sMUSD owner, and switching back returns every token.
